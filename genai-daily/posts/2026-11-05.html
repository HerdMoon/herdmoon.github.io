Can a transformer learn to render Gaussian splats?

GaussianRenderFormer gives a concrete measure of the cost of replacing rasterization. On 300 unseen objects it reaches 38.08 dB PSNR, compared with 44.02 dB for rasterized pruned inputs. The gap makes its decoder experiments especially informative.

Improving planning success rates

The paper introduces motion-guided contrastive regularization for latent world models, which tackles the issue of static collapse that affects action-conditioned latent dynamics. This method enhances representation learning by focusing on motion-related transitions rather than static backgrounds. As a result, it achieves a planning success rate of 78% on OGBench-Cube with randomized backgrounds, a notable increase from 52%. Additionally, it reaches 54% on LIBERO-Object, up from 14%, while maintaining performance on other benchmarks. [1]

World models and control

EvoWorld introduces a novel framework for generative video models that addresses the gap in reasoning about world evolution by implementing the Out-of-Sight Causal Evolution problem. [4]

Geometry and reconstruction

GaussianRenderFormer introduces a transformer-based approach for rendering 3D Gaussian splats without a rasterizer, achieving a PSNR of 38.08 dB on 300 unseen objects, compared to 44.02 dB for rasterized pruned inputs. [9]

AnchorComplete introduces a novel framework for reconstructing complete scene meshes from partial point clouds, significantly improving the fidelity of 3D reconstructions by preserving observed geometry and accommodating varying scene scales. [12]

Solv3R introduces a novel looped transformer architecture for multi-view geometry that allows for continuous refinement of camera poses and scene geometry without the need for task-specific training. [15]

Multimodal learning

NitroFusion2 introduces Text-Stream Adversarial Distillation (TSAD) and Aspect-Cued Discrimination (ACD) to enhance multimodal diffusion transformers (MMDiTs) by utilizing intermediate text-token states for adversarial training. [16]
