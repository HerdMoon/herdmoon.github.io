Reconstructing how a street looked years earlier

CrossTimeEdit uses street views collected across a decade, with 43,653 location-level quadruplets. A convincing historical edit has to reconcile two changes at once: the camera may have moved, and the place itself may have changed.

Historical street-view generation

CrossTimeEdit utilizes the VIGOR-his dataset, which contains 43,653 location-level quadruplets. This dataset supports the model's training and evaluation, allowing for effective historical imagery generation. The model combines supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning to enhance performance, focusing on instruction alignment, background preservation, and quality. This approach enables the generation of more accurate historical street views compared to previous methods, demonstrating the potential of leveraging extensive datasets for generative tasks. [1]

Geometry and reconstruction

T3lescope introduces a novel approach for high-fidelity 3D scene reconstruction from posed multi-view images, eliminating the need for per-scene optimization while maintaining global geometric consistency. [3]

GATOR introduces a novel generative framework for 3D object reconstruction from casual images, achieving high geometric and appearance fidelity while effectively recovering scene-relative poses from sparse observations. [5]

Video-Conditioned Generative Joint 2D-3D Hand Motion Recovery — The paper introduces JoHan, a novel generative framework for recovering 3D hand motion from video sequences without relying on intermediate per-frame pose predictions. [6]

Image synthesis and editing

BudgetPix introduces a compute-adaptive tokenization framework for pixel-space image diffusion, allowing dynamic allocation of computational resources based on visual complexity, which significantly improves the quality-cost tradeoff. [4]
