How do you keep the rest of an edit intact?

OmniXRef accepts text, images and video as references for a video edit. MTEdit studies what happens over a sequence of image edits, building packed trajectories to limit the drift that accumulates between turns.

Multimodal video editing framework

OmniXRef introduces a unified framework for multimodal multi-reference video editing. It allows users to manipulate various attributes of a source video by leveraging text, image, and video references. The model employs a single-stream diffusion transformer and a novel reference-bank interface, achieving superior performance over task-specific baselines and commercial models, as demonstrated by comprehensive evaluations. The framework also includes OmniXRef-Data, a synthetic dataset for training, and OmniXRef-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating video-reference attribute transfer across multiple dimensions. [1]

Image synthesis and editing

The paper introduces MTEdit, a novel multi-turn image editing dataset that utilizes a packed trajectory canvas (PTC) generation pipeline to minimize visual-quality drift across editing turns, resulting in 500K multi-turn trajectories with an average of 5.38 turns. [2]

Can Visual Generation Expand What Unified Multimodal Models Know? — The paper introduces Knowledge Transfer Probing (KTP), a novel protocol that enhances unified multimodal models (UMMs) by incorporating image-generation supervision, leading to improved knowledge transfer from visual to textual modalities. [3]

Geometry and reconstruction

Scene-Designer introduces a novel framework for 3D indoor scene generation that integrates design intelligence through a dual approach of design and critique, utilizing two specialized datasets: Scene-OTA-35K for design learning and Scene-OTC-15K for quality critique. [4]

Learning to Think and Explore in 3D World — The paper introduces ACT3D, a vision-language model that enhances 3D exploration and reasoning by leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) without direct supervision on exploration actions or viewpoints. [7]

World models and control

BridgeWAM introduces a novel Bridge-of-Experts (BoE) architecture that utilizes 32 Latent Bridge Queries (LBQs) to effectively decouple video and action experts, improving action prediction by allowing selective visual-feature use and reducing redundant computation. [5]

The paper introduces GlobeBench, a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate text-based world models across 1,632 tasks in five domains and nineteen simulation skills, addressing the lack of standardized metrics in this area. [8]

Video models and tools

The paper introduces AVC-Reward, an event-centric evaluation framework for assessing audio-video consistency in complex generated scenes, which improves upon traditional metrics by analyzing audio and visual events independently and aggregating results into a three-level consistency metric. [6]
