Joint models for 3D generation and control

Recent methods enhance 3D generation and control through innovative architectures and frameworks. They improve performance in tasks like hand-object interaction, plant reconstruction, and spatial reasoning.

15 collected itemsJump to reading picks ↓ Permalink ↗

3D generation and interaction

"Grasping-the-Future" predicts joint 3D hand-object motion using internet videos, achieving superior performance on 3D datasets through masked spatiotemporal completion and flow matching (1). Similarly, "Plant Orbiter" reconstructs 3D plant morphology from a single image, utilizing video diffusion to enhance accuracy and practicality, achieving the highest 3D F-score compared to existing methods (3). "Click3R" allows users to interactively correct geometric errors in stereo 3D reconstruction with minimal input, significantly reducing reconstruction error (9). InstantHMR enables real-time 3D human mesh recovery on mobile devices, achieving fast performance while maintaining competitive accuracy (10)."},{ [1] [3] [9] [10]

Image synthesis and editing

What kills $v$-prediction? A Patch-wise PCA Perspective on Pixel-Space Flow Matching — The paper introduces the Raw-injected Transformer (RiT), which enhances the effectiveness of $v$-prediction in generative modeling by addressing bandwidth bottlenecks identified through patch-wise PCA analysis. [2]

The paper introduces reComposer, an editable planning interface for image generation that enhances user control by decomposing creative decisions into distinct components such as subject, composition, and color palette. [4]

World models and control

The paper introduces SWAM, a novel approach to scaling World Action Models (WAM) that enhances language-video-action alignment through a comprehensive study of data supervision and model architecture. [5]

The paper introduces $Σ$-LeWM, an enhancement to the LeWorldModel architecture for learning world models in robot control by incorporating two additional latent losses: a backward dynamics predictor and an inverse dynamics action predictor. [6]

The paper introduces the Library of Hands (LoH), a novel training-free method that enhances world model performance by correcting the mapping from commands to actions without retraining the model. [7]

How Many Exposures Does a World Model Need? Learning Unseen Game Mechanics at Test Time — The paper introduces MechEval, a framework for evaluating how many exposures a world model needs to learn mechanics in new environments, and presents WARC, a 35M-parameter vision transformer that updates all parameters after each transition. [11]

The paper introduces Spatial Harness, a novel framework that enhances spatial reasoning in vision-language models (VLMs) by grounding it in executable world states using physics engines. [14]

Multimodal learning

Beyond Modality Preference — The paper introduces a novel intervention technique for multimodal large language models (MLLMs) that addresses the issue of modality conflict by controlling modality arbitration at inference time. [8]

SkinMind introduces a novel multimodal retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) framework that enhances dermatology AI by employing human-inspired graph probing for autonomous evidence seeking. [13]

Models, methods and tools

The paper introduces FewStepRL, a framework that adapts reinforcement learning (RL) objectives for fine-tuning few-step diffusion models, significantly reducing training costs associated with many-step samplers. [12]

Geometry and reconstruction

JoVox introduces a novel discrete flow matching framework for voxel generation that significantly enhances both fidelity and efficiency. [15]
